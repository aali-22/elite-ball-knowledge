# Decisions log

Each entry: the choice, the evidence, the date. Research pages were seen on the date given. Accounts that were studied are not named here.

## 2026-11-23

| Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **League: NBA** for the 14 days | Games most nights, so more attempts and faster feedback than a weekly league |
| **Channels: Instagram, Reddit, Bluesky.** TikTok mirrors the images and is not measured | An Instagram account already existed. Reddit has the most click potential. Bluesky has clickable links and no stated penalty for them. X was skipped: a vendor reports under 100 impressions a post for unpaid accounts ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/), seen 2026-11-23), and small accounts there could not be observed |
| **Reddit is used narrowly**: comments only for the first four days, then at most two original chart posts and two weekly threads, each only after that community's rules have been read | No community's rules could be read during research, so every rule is unconfirmed until read by hand |
| **Accounts switched off for the sprint** | Nothing in the test needs an account, and nothing on the site needs one to read |
| **Indexing switched on at day zero**, once the share card carries the right name | Search takes weeks to show anything, so it cannot disturb a 14-day channel test, and organic visits appear as their own source |
| **Home page wording changed** from "sharpest edges" to "standout findings" | The old wording can be read as something other than analysis. The site is analysis, not advice |
| **Campaign tags come from a fixed list in code**, not free text | The site promises that events carry only short fixed labels. A free-text tag would let any link author make the site report arbitrary text, or a label for one person |
| **Activation is one event**, `activate`, sent once per page load on the first real interaction | One event marked as a key event makes "activated sessions by channel" readable in the standard acquisition report, with no custom exploration |
| **Findings are chosen by hand, never posted automatically** | An automated account posting each night's schedule and results had made 10,347 posts and had 82 followers (observed 2026-11-23) |
| **First audience: analytics hobbyists.** Creators are distributors, not buyers | Hobbyist communities accept original charts with a method note. No evidence was found of creators paying for charts |
| **Lead with standing outliers and the day-after format; tonight's preview is third and is a test** | In 63 posts from 19 accounts (observed 2026-11-23), outliers and explainers were the most common kinds of claim after recaps and roundups (12 each), single-outlier posts did better than rankings or dashboards from accounts of similar size, and "tonight" posts worked when they were about what had just happened |
| **Week one uses phone screenshots** of finding cards, with the site address printed on each card | Cheaper than building image export before knowing which format works |

### Assumptions the research corrected

| Assumption | What the evidence showed |
|---|---|
| Fans will not pay for charts; creators and podcasters might | Fans already pay small monthly amounts for analytics: $5 a month ([cleaningtheglass.com/stats](https://cleaningtheglass.com/stats)) and $9 a month ([Stathead plans](https://info.sports-reference.com/migration/knowledge/subscription-plans)), both seen 2026-11-23. Creators look like distributors rather than buyers |
| A preview of tonight's game is the natural post | Outliers travel better than previews. The closest free neighbour already offers a schedule-adjusted matchup tool for tonight's slate ([macfax.usu.edu/nba](https://macfax.usu.edu/nba), seen 2026-11-23), and percentile-scaled stats are already sold elsewhere, so neither is a difference on its own |
